The stance

Not a scratch copy any more. Whatever ships in its book is the product’s first-run experience, so it has to be deliberate.

Built for other AAC parents

The audience that turns into users, and the one you can speak to honestly. Clinicians are a harder crowd and come second.

They hold the iPad

Design for a stranger poking at it, not for you narrating. Survive that and you survive the other case too.

Two boards, not one

A shareable board with bundled photos for handing over. CJ’s real board stays for when you’re holding it and telling the story.

The main menu

Size does the explaining. The two things you actually touch are large; the four you occasionally need are quiet. Nobody has to read the menu to know what it’s for.

The first run

Welcome, then a book worth having

1

One welcome screen

Two things on it: the child’s name, and a starting board chosen from three big cards — First Words, Yes & No, My People — plus Start Blank. Three is scannable in one look and each is obviously different from the others. Re-runnable later from Settings, which is also how you reset the iPad between showing it to two different parents.

2

Four pages in the book

The board they picked, plus Core Words and Yes / No because almost nobody regrets having those, plus the demo board below. Enough to be useful on day one, small enough to understand in one sitting. Everything else is in the Board Library when they’re ready.

3

One board they can delete

What Talk Tiles Can Do — a real board whose buttons do the thing they describe, rather than a leaflet read aloud. Because it is the app, nothing on it can drift out of date. When they’ve looked, they delete it.

The demo board

Before any of this ships

Six things still open

Five came out of the decisions above. One is a hole the grilling found in the plan itself.

Read the photo licence before bundling anything

Bundling stock photos into a paid app is the ARASAAC problem again. The bar is commercial use allowed and redistribution inside a binary allowed — plenty of “free stock” sites pass the first and fail the second, and Unsplash has tightened its terms before. Check the current wording, and keep a copy of it with the photos.

The welcome can hand you the same board twice

Pick Yes & No as your starting board and the book gives you Yes / No again in the core set. Either drop the duplicate, or make the core set adapt to what was chosen.

Two demo buttons have nowhere to land

“Point at the picture” and “Build a sentence” need a scene page and a keyboard page to jump to — but the book is meant to be four pages. Either they’re hidden pages that don’t appear in page navigation, or the demo board opens them from the Board Library.

Clear the shipping book

Lite currently holds CJ’s real board and two empty “New Board” pages. Export CJ’s board first, then delete all three — if Lite is the real app, that book is what every new user would receive.

Shoot or source about fifteen objects

The hand-over board needs a cat, a cup, a fridge, a door, a toy and so on. Whatever the source, they want to look like a real family’s house rather than a catalogue — that’s the whole point being made.

Decide what Help says now

It survived the round untouched because nothing forced a decision about it. If the demo board explains the app, Help’s job changes — and nobody has looked at what’s in it.

No code was changed. The app on the iPad is exactly as it was after this morning’s reinstall.
